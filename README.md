# AI Yoga Masters

**Ancient practice for intelligent tools.**

AI keeps getting more capable. **AI Yoga Masters asks a different question: can the person using it become more capable too?**

AI Yoga Masters is an open-source, AI-native yoga school founded by **Alex Julian**. Its foundation is the original *AI Yoga Masters* book: a 2024 system that applies yoga, meditation, philosophy, focus training, and practical self-observation to the way people use AI and technology.

> **Train the user, not only the model.**

[Read the original book][book] · [Explore the curriculum](#the-original-curriculum) · [Start with the core practice](#the-core-practice) · [Build with us](#contributing)

**Project status:** Website foundation stage. The original book and curriculum now have a runnable Next.js public-site foundation in this repository. There is not yet a database, user-account system, or AI teaching assistant.

## The thesis

The original book begins by redefining yoga.

It argues that yoga is not primarily about advanced poses or flexibility. Its deeper purpose is **mindfulness, focus, clarity, ethical conduct, and presence**. The physical practice matters, but it supports what the book calls the “yoga of the mind.”

AI Yoga Masters brings that framework into the age of artificial intelligence.

The central idea is simple:

**Better AI use is not only an AI problem. It is also a human-practice problem.**

A more powerful tool does not automatically create a better relationship with that tool. Attention can still fragment. Judgment can still become cloudy. More information can still become overload. A person can still procrastinate, overreact, lose perspective, or become attached to the technology itself.

The book therefore treats yoga as a practical operating system for the person using AI.

Not an escape from technology.

Not worship of technology.

Not rejection of technology.

**Practice for using powerful technology with greater focus, discernment, balance, and freedom.**

## Where this came from

*AI Yoga Masters* was created by Alex Julian in 2024. The book states that Alex had been teaching yoga and meditation for more than a decade and that the intersection of yoga and AI was still new. It also explicitly says that the name **AI Yoga Masters** was intended to be intriguing—not to claim that its creator was a perfected yoga master.

The original system contains **64 numbered lessons across seven chapters**, plus a dedicated collection of yoga flows and meditations. Lessons were designed to be short and individually useful while also fitting into a larger progression.

The book also documents the AI tools used in its original creative process: ChatGPT, Gemini, Suno, and Midjourney. It states that the lesson ideas themselves were Alex Julian’s original work.

This repository is the next stage: preserving the original system while turning it into a public, inspectable, improvable school.

## The core practice

You do not need to complete 64 lessons before AI Yoga Masters becomes useful.

Start with one experiment:

### Use AI with one-pointed attention

1. Choose **one meaningful task**.
2. Decide what you are actually trying to accomplish before prompting.
3. Remove unrelated tabs, feeds, and notifications when practical.
4. Work with the AI on that one task.
5. Notice when your attention leaves the task.
6. Return without drama.
7. Stop when the session stops being useful.
8. Step away from the screen and re-enter the rest of your life.

That is a contemporary application of several themes running through the book: **dharana** (focused attention), **dhyana** (sustained focus), non-attachment, clear intention, and what the book later calls **virtual fasting**.

This eight-step practice is a new synthesis for the public project, not a verbatim exercise from the 2024 book.

The point is not to become rigid.

The point is to become **less divided**.

## The 8 Petals as an AI practice

Chapter 1 places the **8 Petals of Yoga** at the foundation of the system:

| Petal | Book framing | AI-era application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Yamas** | Universal morality | Use powerful tools without harming, stealing, manipulating, or abandoning honesty. |
| **Niyamas** | Personal improvement | Develop the internal conditions that make good judgment possible. |
| **Asana** | Physical practice | Care for the body that is actually doing the work. |
| **Pranayama** | Breath practice | Use the breath to regulate pace, attention, and reactivity. |
| **Pratyahara** | Redirecting focus | Stop feeding every notification, feed, and stimulus your attention. |
| **Dharana** | Cultivating focus | Choose a point of attention and return to it. |
| **Dhyana** | Sustained focus | Stay with worthwhile work long enough to go deep. |
| **Samadhi** | Merging with focus | Experience periods in which the separation between attention and activity falls away. |

The rightmost column is a **new public-project interpretation**. The names and ordering come from the original curriculum.

One of the strongest ideas in the book is that the entire system can be understood as a progressive education in focus.

## Five ideas worth carrying forward

### 1. Upgrade the person

Chapter 2 shifts attention away from asking only what AI can do.

The deeper question is what qualities the person brings to the interaction.

More capability in the machine does not remove the need for patience, discernment, ethics, creativity, emotional regulation, or focus in the human.

### 2. The relationship with the tool matters

The book repeatedly returns to the idea that an object or tool is not the whole story. **The relationship with the object matters.**

That makes AI Yoga Masters neither anti-AI nor blindly pro-AI.

The aim is to develop a relationship in which the person can use the tool deliberately—and can also put it down.

### 3. Meditation should escape the meditation cushion

Chapter 4 introduces **meditationization**: bringing sustained attention into ordinary activity rather than confining meditation to a short formal session.

For an AI-native school, this is especially important.

The practice is not merely:

> meditate for fifteen minutes, then use AI chaotically for ten hours.

The practice is to bring greater awareness into the ten hours too.

### 4. Virtual fasting

The book uses **virtual fasting** for intentionally taking breaks from technology.

This is not a rejection of AI. It is closer to pratyahara: withdrawing attention periodically so that technology does not become the automatic destination of every spare moment.

A healthy AI practice includes periods in which no AI is required.

### 5. Start before you feel finished

The final chapter turns toward implementation, procrastination, and the “sweetness of starting.”

That belongs at the center of an open-source version of the school.

We do not need to design the entire future of AI Yoga Masters before making one useful lesson clearer.

We can practice.

We can build.

We can learn.

Then we can make the next commit.

## A digital yoga practice

The public repository creates a new form of practice:

**Study → Practice → Build → Reflect → Refine → Disconnect**

For example:

- Study one original lesson.
- Practice its underlying principle during a real AI session.
- Notice what changes.
- Turn one useful insight into clearer documentation, curriculum, software, or research.
- Submit the improvement.
- Close the laptop.

This should not become another compulsive productivity system.

There is no spiritual prize for maintaining a GitHub streak.

The repository exists to support the practice—not the other way around.

## What “AI-native” means here

AI-native does **not** mean outsourcing judgment to AI.

It means designing the school with AI understood as part of the environment from the beginning.

That can eventually include:

- structured lessons that AI systems can navigate and cite;
- personalized study paths with transparent boundaries;
- reflective prompts and practice companions;
- curriculum search;
- source-aware question answering;
- accessibility tools;
- multilingual teaching;
- practice journals that remain private by default;
- experiments comparing different human-AI workflows;
- open educational software that other people can inspect and improve.

None of those features should be described as finished until they are actually implemented.

The standard is:

**AI can assist the school. Humans remain responsible for the school.**

## Principles for building this project

The original curriculum gives us a useful engineering philosophy too.

### Ahimsa — do not build harm casually

Consider foreseeable effects. Do not use the language of yoga or AI authority to pressure people, diagnose them, or make unsupported promises.

### Satya — tell the truth about the system

Say what works.

Say what is experimental.

Say what is interpretation.

Say what comes from the book.

Say what comes from evidence.

Say when we do not know.

### Asteya — credit ideas

Do not steal writing, code, research, artwork, teaching systems, or other people’s contributions. Attribution is part of the practice.

### Aparigraha — resist feature accumulation

More is not automatically better. A clean lesson can be more valuable than ten unfinished features.

### Pratyahara — protect attention

The school should not be designed like an engagement trap.

### Dharana — build one thing well

Focus is not only curriculum content. It is a development method.

### Santosha — accept the current version

Acceptance is not complacency. A V0 can be real while still having somewhere to grow.

### Svadhyaya — study the system itself

Notice how we use AI, how the product shapes behavior, and where our own assumptions may be wrong.

## Evidence, interpretation, and revision

The original book combines several different kinds of material:

- classical and modern yoga concepts;
- Alex Julian’s interpretations and lived experience;
- practical exercises;
- philosophy and metaphor;
- health and wellness guidance;
- scientific and quasi-scientific claims;
- speculation about technology and human performance.

Those categories should **not** be silently treated as equivalent.

In particular, chapters 4 and 6 contain claims about earthing, electromagnetic fields, health effects of wireless technology, detoxification, nutrition, sleep, and other wellness topics. Some are presented strongly in the 2024 source text.

The public project should preserve the historical source honestly **while independently reviewing health and scientific claims before turning them into present-day instructional guidance**.

A useful convention for future lessons will be:

- **Source teaching** — what the original book says.
- **Practice** — the exercise being proposed.
- **Evidence** — what credible current research supports.
- **Interpretation** — philosophical or spiritual framing.
- **Open question** — what remains uncertain or contested.

That structure lets the school remain spiritually adventurous without confusing conviction with verification.

## Care and safety

AI Yoga Masters is an educational and contemplative project. It is not medical care, psychotherapy, or a substitute for qualified instruction.

Physical yoga should be adapted to the individual. Advanced postures and inversions are optional, not milestones of worth or mastery. Do not force range of motion, breathing, fasting, or pain tolerance.

For health-related material, especially lessons involving diet, sleep, exercise, injury, pregnancy, mental health, supplements, electromagnetic fields, or other medical questions, use appropriate professional guidance and current evidence.

For AI-related practice, protect private information. Do not paste passwords, API keys, confidential records, private journals, or other people’s sensitive data into public issues or untrusted AI systems.

Mindfulness should increase agency—not create dependence on a teacher, app, repository, or model.

## Contributing

AI Yoga Masters was built around the idea that the system itself should remain open to learning.

If you notice something that can be improved, contribute.

Good contributions include:

- correcting an error;
- improving an explanation;
- adding a credible source;
- distinguishing evidence from interpretation;
- improving accessibility;
- improving curriculum structure;
- building a small, testable feature;
- documenting an experiment;
- questioning an assumption respectfully;
- simplifying something that became unnecessarily complicated.

Use an [issue][issues] for discussion or a pull request for a focused change.

When contributing curriculum, identify whether the material is:

1. from the original 2024 book;
2. an adaptation;
3. a new interpretation;
4. an evidence update;
5. a new practice or experiment.

Do not invent citations, credentials, outcomes, or consensus.

Review AI-generated contributions before submitting them. You are responsible for what you commit.

## Website curriculum edition

`/curriculum` maps all seven chapters and 64 numbered lessons, plus the separate 16-flow and 4-meditation libraries. Chapters 1 and 2’s seventeen lessons are readable on the site; the remaining chapters link to their original PDF sections. Each published lesson page preserves source teaching separately from new AI-assisted practices, reflections, editorial notes, and future verified song/video slots.

Curriculum content lives in `content/curriculum.json`. Original source passages retain their book copyright and are excluded from the code/documentation MIT license; see `content/README.md`. This publication is authorized by the author’s request to make Chapters 1 and 2 readable. Historical and scientific evidence review remains incomplete and is identified on lesson pages.


## Practice Studio

`/practice` guides a six-stage AI session: attention check-in, practice selection, intention, practice, reflection, and closing. One-Pointed Focus is the default, with Clarity & Discernment and Care & Honesty as companion practices. All three link to the original Chapter 1 lessons. Optional 2/5/10/15-minute timers support pause and resume, count wall-clock time across background tabs, and never advance the session automatically. Intention and reflection exist only in React memory; there is no submission or browser-storage persistence. These guided sequences are new applications, separate from the original source teaching.

## Chapter 2 website edition

The Tao of AI (Lessons 10–17) is readable on the site with original source paragraphs, accurate printed/PDF page references, and separately labeled contemporary practices, reflections, and editorial notes. Lesson navigation continues across the Chapter 1/2 boundary and points to Chapter 3 after Lesson 17. Published lessons link to the Practice Studio.
